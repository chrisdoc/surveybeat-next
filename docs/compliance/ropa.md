# Record of Processing Activities (RoPA) — GDPR Article 30

**Status:** Baseline Governance Record (Release 1 / Release 2 implemented scope)  
**Owner:** SurveyBeat Product & Infrastructure Operators  
**Review cadence:** At least annually, and upon any SQL migration (`packages/cloudflare/migrations/`, `packages/cloudflare/d1-migrations/`), retention change (`packages/domain/src/Policy.ts`), or sub-processor change (`docs/infra.md`, `docs/compliance/vendor-dpa-register.md`).  
**Vocabulary:** Domain terms follow [`GLOSSARY.md`](../../GLOSSARY.md) (`Organization`, `Person`, `Organization admin`, `Team`, `Membership`, `Lead`, `Pulse`, `Round`, `Invitation`, `Participation record`, `Submission`, `Answer`, `Edit key`, `Result`, `Redaction`, `Comment theme`, `Discussion guide`, `Action`, `Note`, `Minimum group size`).

---

## 1. Controller vs. Processor Role Split

SurveyBeat acts in two distinct GDPR capacities depending on the processing activity:

1. **Data Controller (Art. 30(1)):** For marketing site delivery, sign-in identity and session management (`Better Auth` in D1), `Organization` creation allowlisting and global routing indexes, and platform-level operational security/disaster-recovery backups.
2. **Data Processor (Art. 30(2)):** On behalf of each customer `Organization` (the Controller for its workforce directory and Team check-ins) for directory/Team management, `Pulse` and `Round` orchestration, `Invitation` delivery, `Participation record` tracking, `Submission` ingestion, `Result` computation, optional AI `Comment theme` and `Discussion guide` generation, and `Action`/`Note` tracking.

Every `Organization`'s domain data is stored in an isolated **Cloudflare Durable Object with SQLite storage** (`Organization`). The shared **Cloudflare D1 database** (`Database`) stores only authentication state, email bookkeeping, and minimal global routing indexes—never `Pulse`, `Round`, `Submission`, `Answer`, or `Result` content (`docs/infra.md`).

## 3. Anonymity and residual identifiability of `submission` and `answer` rows (GDPR Recital 26)

Under GDPR Article 4(1) and Recital 26, data is outside the scope of personal data protection if it is rendered anonymous in such a manner that the data subject is not or is no longer identifiable, taking account of **"all the means reasonably likely to be used, such as singling out, either by the controller or by another person to identify the natural person directly or indirectly."**

The `submission` and `answer` tables (`packages/cloudflare/migrations/0008_submissions.sql`) are built so a stored row has no column that identifies a `Person`. That structural split is test-verified (`Submissions.privacy.test.ts`, `Unlinkability.privacy.test.ts`). It is not a claim that every stored comment is non-personal data. A comment can still identify its author through a unique anecdote that rule-based `Redaction` does not remove. Where that happens, treat the comment as personal data for retention and for rights requests. The controls below are what the schema and the ingestion path actually do:

1. **Structural decoupling in schema (`0008_submissions.sql`, `0007_invitations.sql`):**
   - `submission` contains only `(id, round_id, edit_key_hash)` and `answer` contains only `(submission_id, question_version_id, value, comment)`.
   - `participation` contains only `(round_id, person_id)`.
   - Neither `submission`, `answer`, nor `participation` has any timestamp column—going stricter than SPEC §5.3's coarse day bucket (ADR 0005).
   - No `submission`, `answer`, or `participation` table stores a `locale` or language column, preventing language-based singling out in small `Team`s (`Submissions.privacy.test.ts` A10).
   - All three tables are declared `WITHOUT ROWID`, and `submission.id` is a random 128-bit hex key (`secureRandomBytes` in `SqlRounds.ts`), eliminating SQLite insertion-order `rowid` correlation (`Submissions.privacy.test.ts` A1, A2).
2. **Zero-observability ingestion boundary (`SubmissionWorker.ts`, `SubmissionRoutes.ts`, ADR 0005):**
   - Answer links put the `Invitation` token in the URL fragment (`#t=`), so opening the answer page does not send it. `GET /delivery?token=` and `GET /api/reminders/unsubscribe?t=` on `MainWorker` still send that token in the query string, where Workers Logs and `Referer` can see it (issue #311).
   - `SubmissionWorker` accepts credentials only in the POST JSON body and rejects any request carrying both an `Invitation` token and an `Edit key` (`SubmissionRoutes.privacy.test.ts` A5).
   - `SubmissionWorker` has `observability.enabled: false`, `logs.enabled: false`, `traces.enabled: false`, `logpush: false`, `tailConsumers: []`, and `streamingTailConsumers: []`, verified on every stage by `pnpm check:observability` (`infra/scripts/check-observability.ts`) and `pnpm test:preview-privacy` (`infra/tests/privacy/canary-sweep.ts`).
   - `SubmissionWorker` never returns stored `Answer`s for an `Edit key` (`SubmissionRoutes.privacy.test.ts`).
3. **Store-time `Redaction` (ADR 0009, `packages/domain/src/privacy/Redaction.ts`):**
   - Before any `Answer` comment is written to SQLite, `recordFirstAnswer` and `saveAnswer` (`SqlRounds.ts`) call `redact` (`Redaction.ts`) inside the `Organization` Durable Object. `SubmissionWorker` forwards the raw answers over Durable Object RPC and does not redact them. The roster is the directory's `PersonRecord` list: email, and no display name. `redact` strips email local parts (split on `.`, `_`, and `-`), email addresses, phone numbers, `@handles`, and URLs, replacing them with `[name]`, `[email]`, `[phone]`, `[handle]`, and `[link]`. A candidate shorter than 2 characters is skipped, so a one-character local part can remain when it appears as a word. `+` and `%` are word breaks for the matcher and are not splits, so a local part that contains either can remain even when it is at least 2 characters. A full email address is still replaced. A display name that is not part of an email local part is not removed.
   - Raw comment text exists solely in transient request memory and is never persisted or logged (`Submissions.privacy.test.ts` A4b).
4. **Statistical unlinkability & disclosure controls (`Unlinkability.privacy.test.ts`, `Results.privacy.test.ts`, `Themes.privacy.test.ts`):**
   - `Unlinkability.privacy.test.ts` and `Submissions.privacy.test.ts` (A4) prove that dumping `participation` and `submission` in key order yields no better than random-guess linkage across `rank`, `keyOrder`, `rowid`, and `sharedColumns` attacks.
   - `Minimum group size` (`>= 3`, default `4`, ADR 0004) blocks any `Result`, `Question` score, `Dimension` score, or subtraction-derived sub-group below `N`; no live `Result`s are shown while a `Round` is `Open`; `Lead` comments are unlinked from ratings and `Submission` order; `Comment theme`s require `>= 2` supporting comments and pass a 6-word anti-quote filter (ADR 0016, ADR 0019).
5. **Survival upon erasure (`Submissions.privacy.test.ts` A9):**
   - `directory.removePerson` is Remove from Organization, a soft delete. It sets `person.removed_at` and `membership.ended_at`, deletes that `Person`'s `participation` rows and `test_send`s, revokes `invitation`s, and sets `person_index.removed_at` in D1. The index row stays so an access export can find the `Organization`; sign-in queries require `removed_at IS NULL`. The `person` row (with its address), membership history, `round_member`, `pulse_reminder_opt_out` and `email_send` rows stay until an erasure.
   - `directory.erasePerson` (an `Organization admin`) and `me.erasePerson` (the signed-in `Person`) are Erase personal data (issue 307, ADR 0027). Both need the `Person`'s address typed as confirmation. Inside the `Organization` Durable Object, `person.email` becomes `erased-<personId>@erased.invalid`; `auth_user_id`, `time_zone`, `locale` and `detected_locale` become `NULL`; `organization_admin` becomes `0`; `removed_at` and `erased_at` are set; every active `membership` ends; and `participation`, `round_member`, `invitation`, `pulse_reminder_opt_out`, `test_send`, the `email_send` rows addressed to the `Person` (and their `email_outbox` rows) are deleted. An `AddedToTeam` email to someone else that names the `Person` as `leadName` gets the tombstone address instead. In D1 the `person_index`, `invitation_index`, `test_invitation_index` rows for that `Person`, the `email_message` and non-production `captured_email` rows of the deleted sends, and the `organization_claim` for that `Organization` are deleted. When the human has no `person_index` row left, `auth_session`, `auth_session_organization`, `auth_account`, `auth_passkey`, `auth_member`, `auth_invitation`, `auth_verification` and `auth_user` are deleted too, which ends every session at once. `me.eraseAccount` does the same for a human with no `Person`. A sole `Lead` or sole `Organization admin` is refused until the role is handed over. `action.owner_id` stays and the app shows a former member. `submission`, `answer` and `round_result` rows are never touched, so they survive erasure, and a comment that still identifies its author remains personal data.

### Residual Caveats Documented for Transparency

- **Free-text self-identification:** `redact` strips email local parts of at least 2 characters from the roster, plus emails, phones, handles, and URLs, when the word matcher can see them. It does not strip a one-character local part when it appears as a word, a local part that contains `+` or `%` (those are word breaks, and the candidate list does not split on them), a display name that is absent from those local parts, a nickname, or a unique anecdote. The answer page says what is and is not removed (`apps/web/src/messages/answer.ts` `answer.comment.disclosure`). The in-app Privacy page has a separate `privacy.comments.body` in `apps/web/src/messages/catalog.ts`.
- **30-day Point-in-Time Recovery (PITR) window (ADR 0005):** Replaying the Durable Object write-ahead log (WAL) during a PITR restore could correlate writes made in the same transaction. SurveyBeat mitigates this through strict operator-only access controls, a hard 30-day backup expiry (`Policy.backupRetentionDays = 30`), and explicit user-facing disclosure (`catalog.ts` `privacy.restore`, `privacy.restoreTrigger`).
